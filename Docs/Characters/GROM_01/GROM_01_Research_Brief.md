Status labels used below: **VERIFIED** (multiple credible sources agree) · **PLAUSIBLE** (consistent with documented practice, single-source or inferred) · **UNCERTAIN** (evidence conflicts or gaps) · **UNSUPPORTED** (no evidence found — must not be used).

## A. Character purpose

GROM_01 is the project's **first playable soldier character** — a fictional Soviet-side
viewpoint operative for our stealth-oriented first-person game. The name "GROM"
(Гром, "Thunder") echoes the historical KGB assault group designation from the
December 1979 Kabul operation, but **GROM_01 is an internal production name only** and
must never be presented as proof that our fictional character belonged to any real
unit. The character is a fictional person in a historically grounded setting; the
December 1979 Tajbeg Palace events are *background context* — this task does not
create that mission, the palace, or any encounters.

## C. Clothing research summary

### C.1 Soviet regular-army baseline (context — NOT our character's default look)
- **M69 everyday field uniform**: khaki cotton, closed collar jacket with patch pockets, straight trousers; winter variant (padded, wadded jacket); tropical variant existed.
- **SSh-68** steel helmet (olive; occasionally repainted sand in-country later).
- Boots: black leather lace-up or Kirza boots; 1980s lace-up M88 boots came later (**UNCERTAIN** for Dec 1979 exact footwear of special groups).
- **No Afghanka**. **No 6B2 vest** (Dec 1979). Webbing: standard Soviet belts/pouches (AKM magazine pouches), RD-54 rucksack **PLAUSIBLE** for spetsnaz from 1980 onward, **UNCERTAIN** for Dec 1979.
- Weapons: AKM/AKMS (7.62) and AK-74/AKS-74 (5.45) mixed; RPK/RPK-74, PKM, SVD, RPG-7, RGD-5/F-1 grenades; PM pistol; suppressed **PB (6P9)** and **APB** pistols for KGB work.

### C.2 Afghan disguise configuration (the documented special-groups practice)
- **Afghan national men's dress**: loose **perahan tunba** (tunic-and-trousers) in blues/browns/greens/greys; **chapan** (quilted striped robe) for December cold; **pakol** wool roll-hat ("pushtunka" in spetsnaz slang). Spetsnaz reconnaissance in Afghan dress is documented practice across 1979–89; Central Asian personnel blended in best.
- **Afghan army uniform variant** for Storm-333 night work: DRA army fatigues, specially manufactured, plus **white armbands** for friend/foe identification in darkness.
- Equipment carried *over or under* the disguise was minimized; suppressed sidearms and folded-stock rifles favored for concealment.

### C.3 Regular-vs-special differences (important)
Special-purpose personnel (KGB Directorate S groups, GRU spetsnaz) differed from line infantry: no visible insignia, Afghan or DRA clothing instead of M69, suppressed and compact weapons, lighter/less standardized webbing, and selection for Central Asian appearance or language training. **Line-infantry equipment must not be copied onto our special-groups character.**

## F. Proposed visual direction (ONE starting direction)

**GROM_01 — "The Case Officer's Shadow" (December 1979, Kabul, pre-assault disguise configuration):**
A fictional Soviet operative presented as an Afghan civilian of Central Asian appearance:

- **Head**: pakol wool roll-hat; dark scarf; short Central Asian beard/stubble.
- **Body**: cream/grey **perahan tunic**, dark **chapan** (quilted striped robe) open over it — December Kabul winter.
- **Legs**: loose perahan trousers; **plain leather boots** (local style) — avoids Soviet-identifying footwear.
- **Gear (minimal, concealed)**: a simple across-the-shoulder cloth bandolier or worn satchel; folded-stock **AKMS** carried in a blanket roll or local cloth sling; **PB suppressed pistol** in a trouser pocket (silhouette only).
- **No insignia, no helmet, no Soviet webbing visible.**
- **Silhouette target**: indistinguishable from an Afghan townsman at 10 m — which is the historically documented *point* of the disguise.

Why this direction: (1) the disguise practice is **PLAUSIBLE-to-VERIFIED** for these units; (2) simple draped cloth is **beginner-pipeline friendly** (marvelous-designer-friendly, no complex hard-surface gear); (3) it sidesteps every insignia/identity problem; (4) it is visually distinct from the future regular-army characters (built-in art direction contrast).

*Variant B (later, optional)*: the documented **night-assault look** — Afghan army fatigues + white armband + SSh-68 — useful for a mission-specific appearance, historically verified for Storm-333.
